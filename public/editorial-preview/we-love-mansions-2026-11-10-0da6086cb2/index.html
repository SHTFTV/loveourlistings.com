We Love This Listing: A Seattle Tudor With a Collected Character

A 1924 Washington Park home pairs hand-waxed woodwork and European finds with expansive water views.

Unpublished editorial preview — awaiting visual QA. Original source date: 2026-11-10.

A Tudor with layers

Robb Report identifies this gated home at 530 36th Avenue E in Seattle’s Washington Park as a 1924 Tudor with 7,910 square feet of living space. Its architectural age provides the starting point; what interests us is how later additions give that history another layer rather than treating it as a finished composition.

A renovation shaped by collecting

According to the publication, Jonathan and Elizabeth Roberts undertook a major remodel between 2000 and 2004, incorporating pieces sourced across Europe, including a stone archway from Ireland installed in the gardens. That approach suggests a home assembled around particular objects, not simply a coordinated decorative scheme.

Woodwork sets the tone

Robb Report describes hand-waxed woodwork in the main living areas, pecan floorboards in the dining room and cherry paneling in the office. To us, that variety of timber is a compelling counterpoint to the primary bathroom’s reported glass vessel sinks and carved stone tub: traditional craftsmanship alongside more contemporary forms.

A different perspective outdoors

The report also notes a half-moon bench overlooking the water, with the Cascades beyond, and a landscaped rooftop deck offering a broader outlook. These are two distinct ways to frame the setting—one centered on a seat, the other on an elevated vantage point.
