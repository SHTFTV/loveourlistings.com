We Love This Listing: A Seattle Tudor With a Collector’s Point of View

A 1924 home in Washington Park pairs substantial proportions with European finds and an unusually rich palette of woodwork, according to Robb Report.

Unpublished editorial preview — awaiting visual QA. Original source date: 2026-11-10.

Scale with a historical anchor

Robb Report identifies the gated Tudor at 530 36th Avenue E as a five-bedroom, seven-bathroom residence spanning 7,910 square feet. Those are mansion-sized numbers, but the more compelling editorial thread is how a century-old architectural framework accommodates a distinctly personal collection.

A renovation built around discoveries

According to the publication, owners Jonathan and Elizabeth Roberts undertook a major remodeling between 2000 and 2004, incorporating pieces sourced across Europe. A stone archway from Ireland found its place in the gardens. To us, that intersection of architecture and collecting is the feature’s strongest idea: renovation as composition rather than wholesale replacement.

Woodwork supplies the continuity

The report describes hand-waxed woodwork in the main living areas, pecan floorboards in the dining room and a cherry-paneled office. Read together, those material choices suggest a useful counterpoint to the collected antiques—a shared vocabulary of timber rather than a succession of unrelated showpieces.

The landscape gets a final word

Robb Report also notes a half-moon bench overlooking the water, with the Cascades beyond, and a landscaped rooftop deck offering a broader view. The appeal is the contrast: rooms organized around crafted surfaces, followed by outdoor spaces whose principal subject is the landscape.
