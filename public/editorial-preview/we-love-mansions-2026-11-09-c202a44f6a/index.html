We Love This Listing: A 1911 Venice Beach House With Layers of Character

A triple-lot estate near the beach pairs a boarding-house past with timber ceilings, distinctive fireplaces and generous gathering spaces.

Unpublished editorial preview — awaiting visual QA. Original source date: 2026-11-09.

A history beyond the headline

According to Robb Report, this Venice Beach property was built in 1911 for Warren Wilson, founder of the Los Angeles Daily Journal. It later became a boarding house whose guests included Charlie Chaplin and Abbot Kinney. Those connections are compelling, but the architectural continuity makes the stronger editorial case.

Character in the materials

Robb Report identifies retained hardwood floors, wood-beam ceilings on both levels, and decorative tile and brick fireplaces. We love the combination: several distinct materials carrying the history, rather than one period detail doing all the work.

Rooms with individual identities

The publication reports nine bedrooms and seven bathrooms, describing a plaid-wallpapered bedroom with a private balcony and another bedroom paired with a navy-blue sitting room. The appeal here is individuality rather than uniformity—a fitting quality for a house with such a layered past.

Shared spaces, inside and out

Robb Report describes a wood-paneled living area with a working wood-burning fireplace, a dining banquette curving into a bay window, and a brick garden walkway connecting a deck and covered porch. To us, these varied gathering spaces are the feature’s quiet counterweight to its famous-name history.
