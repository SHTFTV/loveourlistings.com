We Love This Listing: A London Water Tower With a Room Inside the Tank

A Victorian utility structure becomes a vertically arranged home, with a glass extension below and a city-view retreat above.

Unpublished editorial preview — awaiting visual QA. Original source date: 2026-11-10.

Civic History, Domestic Scale

According to Robb Report, this London tower was built in the 1860s to supply the nearby Lambeth Workhouse and converted into a roughly 4,500-square-foot residence in 2011. The architectural appeal lies in that change of purpose: infrastructure designed around storage now accommodates everyday life.

A Deliberate Contrast

Robb Report describes Venetian Gothic brickwork paired with a glass-and-steel extension called the Cube. Its ground floor contains an open-plan kitchen and dining area alongside a reception room with full-height sliding glass doors. Rather than disguise the addition as Victorian, the design lets the two architectural languages remain distinct.

Living Vertically

The report identifies an original York stone staircase connecting the floors, plus a separate elevator tower. That pairing is particularly compelling in a conversion of this kind: the historic circulation remains part of the home's identity, while the newer intervention offers another way through its unusual vertical plan.

The Tank Becomes the Destination

At the top, Robb Report places the Prospect Room inside the original cast-iron water tank, with windows on all four sides and views including the London Eye. This is the conversion's strongest idea: the vessel that once held water becomes a room defined by its outward outlook.
