We Love This Listing: A London Water Tower With a Room Inside Its Original Tank

Venetian Gothic brickwork, a glass-and-steel addition and a former water tank give this London conversion an unusually legible architectural story.

Unpublished editorial preview — awaiting visual QA. Original source date: 2026-11-10.

Infrastructure, Reconsidered

According to Robb Report, this tower was built in the 1860s to supply the nearby Lambeth Workhouse and converted into a roughly 4,500-square-foot residence in 2011. The editorial appeal lies in that change of purpose: a building designed to serve a neighbourhood has become a home without losing its defining form.

A Deliberate Contrast

Robb Report describes Venetian Gothic brickwork paired with an ultramodern glass-and-steel extension called the Cube. Its ground floor contains an open-plan kitchen and dining room alongside a reception room. Rather than making the addition imitate the tower, this approach gives the domestic spaces a distinctly different architectural language.

The Vertical Thread

An original York stone staircase connects all floors, while a separate elevator tower provides another route, the publication reports. That pairing is especially interesting in a conversion organised vertically: the historic circulation remains part of the design, with a newer alternative alongside it.

The Tank Becomes the Destination

The strongest idea is at the top. Robb Report places the Prospect Room inside the original cast-iron water tank, with windows on all four sides and views including the London Eye. Reusing the tank as a room makes the tower’s former function central to its new identity—not merely a historical footnote.
