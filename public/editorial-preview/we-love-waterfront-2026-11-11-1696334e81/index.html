We Love This Listing: A Kona Coast Home Composed Around Courtyards

Olson Kundig’s pavilion-based Hawaii residence makes privacy, volcanic terrain and carefully framed views part of the architectural plan.

Unpublished editorial preview — awaiting visual QA. Original source date: 2026-11-11.

A house in several parts

According to Robb Report, Olson Kundig’s 6,500-square-foot Kona Coast residence comprises flat-roofed pavilions arranged around gardens and courtyards. What interests us is the balance between separation and connection: rather than concentrating everything beneath one roof, the design gives the spaces between buildings a meaningful role.

Views with a sequence

Robb Report describes a nine-foot-tall slatted wood screen concealing the front door. Beyond it, the entrance route opens across a courtyard toward a cinder cone and the ocean. To us, that progression is more compelling than an uninterrupted panorama: the view becomes a gradual discovery.

Privacy beyond the bedroom

The publication reports that the primary suite occupies its own building, linked to the main house by a covered walkway, and that every bedroom opens onto a private courtyard with an outdoor shower. Our takeaway is that privacy here is an organizing principle, not simply a matter of closing a door.

Materials that belong to the setting

Robb Report identifies concrete walls, wood ceilings, cedar screens and basalt floors, with deep roof overhangs providing shade. We find the basalt particularly persuasive as a design choice: it establishes a material relationship with the surrounding lava without requiring the architecture to imitate the landscape.
