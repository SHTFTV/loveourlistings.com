We Love This Listing: A Park Avenue Penthouse Composed for Company

Clive Davis’s Ritz Tower duplex pairs a nearly 48-foot great room with a screening room and a distinctly two-level approach to entertaining, according to Robb Report.

Unpublished editorial preview — awaiting visual QA. Original source date: 2026-11-11.

Two Apartments, One Ambitious Home

Robb Report traces the penthouse’s creation to Davis’s purchase of a full-floor apartment in 1985 and the unit below in 1989, followed by a collaboration with designers Vicente Wolf and Robert Metzger. Our take: the combination gives this home a more interesting premise than size alone.

A Great Room with a Musical Past

According to the publication, a private elevator landing opens onto the upper-level great room, where Davis kept his Steinway grand piano and hosted gatherings. At nearly 48 feet long, the room suggests an unusually generous setting for conversation and performance—not merely a place to arrange sofas.

A Different Register Downstairs

Robb Report places a formal dining room, a 12-seat Art Deco-style screening room and a larger secondary kitchen on the lower level, which has its own entrance. Editorially, that separation is the compelling move: dinner and a film have their own territory within the duplex.

Materials That Hold the Composition Together

The publication describes white oak herringbone-patterned floors alongside Macassar ebony paneling, cabinetry and bookcases. We love the contrast on paper: patterned oak and dark wood offer a decorative counterpoint to the home's expansive entertaining spaces, giving the feature a material story as well as a musical one.
