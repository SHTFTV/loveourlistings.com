We Love This Listing: Havilland Hall’s Classical Grandeur in Guernsey

An 1830 country house near St Peter Port pairs a columned façade with an expansive orangery and an estate ‘village’.

Published 2026-11-16. Original source date: 2026-11-16.

With thanks

Country Life · Savills

First published by Country Life. Love Our Listings wrote this feature from that account.

A façade with familiar proportions

Country Life dates Havilland Hall’s completion to 1830 and places it in Guernsey’s Vauquiedor valley near St Peter Port. The publication compares its neo-Classical frontage with the north façade of the White House, citing four pillars, a pediment and iron balconies. The architectural appeal lies in that disciplined composition, rather than the famous resemblance alone.

Space beyond ceremony

According to Country Life, a vestibule opens into a grand hallway with a curving staircase and galleried landing. Alongside the drawing and formal dining rooms, the publication describes a snug with a separate bar. That contrast is compelling: a house with ceremonial scale also makes room for a less formal register.

An extended southern edge

Country Life reports an orangery stretching more than 50 feet along the house’s south side, plus a modern addition containing an indoor pool with a glass ceiling, gym and spa rooms. Editorially, the orangery is the standout counterpoint to the formal frontage—a long, light-oriented space rather than another statement reception room.

An estate in several parts

The publication describes 80 acres encompassing formal gardens, lawns and woodland, alongside an area called ‘the village’ with five separate cottages and other estate buildings. That arrangement gives Havilland Hall a broader identity than its mansion alone: the interest is in the relationship between a commanding main house and its smaller-scale surroundings.
