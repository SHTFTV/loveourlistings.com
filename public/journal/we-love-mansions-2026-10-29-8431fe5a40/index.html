We Love This Listing: A Farmyard Reimagined as a Family Home

In Country Life, garden designer James Alexander-Sinclair recalls the six-bedroom country house he built within a former farmyard—and later sold.

Published 2026-11-12. Original source date: 2026-10-29.

A courtyard as the starting point

Alexander-Sinclair describes finding a concrete courtyard with tumble-down barns beside his parents-in-law’s home. The appeal here is the transformation: an agricultural site became the setting for a family house, with proximity to grandparents helping to shape the decision.

Six bedrooms, one clear footprint

According to his account, the new house was L-shaped, with six bedrooms, all within the original courtyard. That relationship between a substantial house and an existing footprint offers a more particular architectural story than size alone.

Salvage and compromise

He reports dismantling the old structures to reuse their bricks and roof slates, although the gathered slates were stolen. He also recalls economising on radiators after the house exceeded their budget. Those admissions give the project a refreshingly unvarnished character: ambition came with practical trade-offs.

The connection beyond the wall

One detail stands out in his recollection: a gap in the garden wall allowed access to his wife’s parents’ house and the woods. He says the family sold after the children left home. This is a retrospective portrait, not a claim of current availability; its strongest theme is a home shaped around a particular chapter of family life.
