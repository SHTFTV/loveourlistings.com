We Love This Listing: At Sword Gate House, the harbour comes into the dining room

A painted view of Charleston survives inside this early nineteenth-century house in the South of Broad neighbourhood.

Published 2026-11-25. Original source date: 2026-11-20.

With thanks

Nick Cann with ArchPresence via ELLE Decor

First published by ELLE Decor. Love Our Listings wrote this feature from that account.

An ironworker left the house its name

Start at the street, where Christopher Werner’s wrought-iron gates still mark the entrance. The gardens beyond occupy about an acre. Then look inside the dining room, where an original mural brings a view of the historic harbour to the table. Between them, the gates and the painting preserve two very different ways the city figures in the design of a single address.

A house scaled for gatherings

A three-level piazza gives the house several vantage points over the grounds, while the ballroom provides space to gather indoors. In Nick Cann’s interior photograph, a piano stands beside tall windows. Dark walls throw the pale shutters and their deep surrounds into relief. ELLE Decor described the preserved millwork as part of interior designer Cortney Bishop’s renovation and reported a $35 million asking price through Maison Real Estate on November 20.
