LAND & LEGACY

The country house should come with a plan. Not just a hat.

Ranches, acreage and the appealing business of making room for a different life.

LOVE OUR LISTINGS EDITORIAL · OCTOBER 19, 2026

Country property has a persuasive vocabulary: space, quiet, room to breathe. The practical vocabulary is worth learning too. Access. Water. Buildings. Maintenance. What, exactly, will all that land do?

A private retreat and a working ranch can share an attractive gate while asking very different things of their owner. Horses, agricultural use, a workshop and a large garden each change the brief. It helps to separate an actual ambition from something that merely looks excellent in a brochure.

We would start with an ordinary week. Who is there? What needs attention? Which buildings earn their place? Is the dream to work the land, invite a crowd, or finally have a long view with no one looking back?

The pleasure is not diminished by practical questions. It becomes more believable. Bring the intended uses to advisers who know the property and the area, and establish what is permitted and included. Then buy the hat if you like. It should be one of the smaller decisions.
