We Love This Listing: A Kona Coast Home Composed Around Lava and Courtyards

Olson Kundig’s pavilion-style residence pairs ocean-facing outdoor spaces with a remarkably deliberate approach to privacy, shade and volcanic terrain.

Published 2026-11-12. Original source date: 2026-11-11.

A collection of smaller worlds

According to Robb Report, Olson Kundig designed this 6,500-square-foot Kona Coast residence as flat-roofed pavilions arranged around gardens and courtyards. The appeal is architectural rather than simply expansive: breaking the home into separate structures makes the spaces between buildings feel as important as the rooms themselves.

Views that unfold

Robb Report describes a nine-foot-tall slatted wood screen concealing the front door, followed by a view across the courtyard toward a cinder cone and the ocean beyond. That sequence is the compelling idea here: the landscape becomes a gradual reveal, rather than a single panorama delivered at the threshold.

Privacy with an outdoor connection

The primary suite occupies its own building, linked to the main house by a covered walkway, the publication reports. Each bedroom also opens onto a private courtyard with an outdoor shower. To us, those individual outdoor spaces are a particularly thoughtful counterpoint to the shared gathering areas.

Materials in conversation with the site

Robb Report identifies concrete walls, wood ceilings, cedar screens and basalt stone floors, with deep roof overhangs providing shade. Our favorite aspect is the restrained material vocabulary: it offers a way to read the architecture alongside the lava landscape without treating that rugged setting as mere scenery.
