We Love This Listing: The Squirrel House keeps an art collector’s garden in view

A garden wall and a gallery wall share the light at Barbara Jakobson’s former East 74th Street home.

Published 2026-11-21. Original source date: 2026-11-20.

An invitation to look up

In the photograph of the living room, artwork reaches up one wall and a black grid divides the glass facing the garden. Through those panes a guest could keep an eye on the courtyard while talking to someone on the sofa. A skylight brings daylight in from another direction. It is easy to see how this room lent itself to conversation. The Spaces reported on November 20 that Christie’s International Real Estate had listed the townhouse for $8.995 million.

Spot the squirrels from the sidewalk

Collector Barbara Jakobson bought the house in 1965, nine years after architect John Kingsland enlarged it. Among the visitors she welcomed here were artist Jasper Johns and writer Philip Roth. The house has eight bedrooms across five storeys, but some of its most memorable art is visible without setting foot indoors. Bronze squirrels made by Jane Canfield accompany the red lacquered entrance doors. They explain the name by which neighbours came to know the place.
