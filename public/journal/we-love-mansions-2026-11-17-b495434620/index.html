We Love This Listing: A Wiltshire Farmhouse Rethinking What Lies Behind Tradition

At West Swainsford Farm near Mere, a reported 3,000sq ft farmhouse pairs traditional lime-rendered design with hemp-based construction and an ambitious energy specification.

Published 2026-11-17. Original source date: 2026-11-17.

With thanks

Country Life · Alamy

First published by Country Life. Love Our Listings wrote this feature from that account.

A farmhouse with a different brief

Country Life reports that the farmhouse at West Swainsford Farm, near Mere in Wiltshire, is a collaboration between Hemspan and the Duchy of Cornwall. The article quotes Duchy sustainability officer Sam Henderson describing the 3,000sq ft project as a research and development exercise. For us, that experimental brief is its defining attraction.

Traditional does not mean conventional

Architect Alex Hibbert-Job tells Country Life that the house was designed as a traditional, lime-rendered Duchy farmhouse rather than a modernist structure. Our interest lies in that distinction: architectural familiarity need not rule out a different approach to construction.

The work behind the walls

According to Country Life, the timber-frame construction uses a panel system manufactured off-site. Hibbert-Job describes its intended performance as bordering on passive-house thermal efficiency. That is a reported design assessment, rather than a claim of passive-house certification.

An energy strategy worth examining

Country Life lists a 13-kilowatt solar system, battery storage, an air-source heat pump and mechanical ventilation with heat recovery. It also describes an underground-air Canadian well system. Editorially, the compelling idea is the combination: this is a farmhouse whose interest extends beyond its outward style to how its systems are intended to work together.
