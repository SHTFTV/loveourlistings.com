Why we chose it

A penthouse does not have to be an exercise in collecting extra rooms. Sometimes its appeal is a much more precise change in the everyday: another way to open a door, find a little distance from the street and make the outside part of the home. That is the question behind our penthouse pick at 76 Madison Avenue in NoMad. The editorial interest is the relationship between the rooms and the terrace, rather than a claim that a higher floor automatically means a better life.

The offering

Brown Harris Stevens advertises PH2 for US$5,995,000. The broker describes approximately 1,900 square feet with two bedrooms, two full bathrooms and a powder room, plus an 850-square-foot private wraparound terrace. Its description gives the home north, east and west exposures and says French doors connect each room to the terrace. We have not independently measured those areas or inspected the home; the figures are attributed listing information.

Follow the thresholds.

Watch the movement

For a reader watching the broker's original film, a useful viewing exercise is to follow the thresholds. Does the outside feel like somewhere you would go occasionally, or part of the route through the day? Consider where morning coffee, a quiet phone call or an evening with friends would happen. Those are questions to bring to a showing, not promises that a film can answer for everyone. They make an architectural feature easier to think about than a string of superlatives does.

The ownership conversation

That is also why we would spend time on proportion rather than simply counting amenities. Two bedrooms can be a deliberate choice, and a generous outdoor edge can change the way a comparatively focused plan reads. We like homes that offer something specific to discuss. Here, the conversation is about how much of a city residence can feel connected to open air, without pretending the city has vanished below it.
