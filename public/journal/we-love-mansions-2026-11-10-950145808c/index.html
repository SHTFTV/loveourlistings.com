We Love This Listing: A London Water Tower With a New Perspective

Victorian brickwork, a glass-and-steel addition and a former water tank turned lookout give this London conversion its distinctive architectural rhythm.

Published 2026-11-12. Original source date: 2026-11-10.

Infrastructure, Reconsidered

According to Robb Report, this tower was built in the 1860s to supply the nearby Lambeth Workhouse and converted into a roughly 4,500-square-foot residence in 2011. Its appeal lies in that dramatic change of purpose: an imposing piece of civic infrastructure rethought at a domestic scale.

A Deliberate Contrast

Robb Report describes Venetian Gothic brickwork paired with an ultramodern glass-and-steel extension called the Cube. Its ground floor contains an open-plan kitchen and dining room alongside a reception room with full-height sliding glass doors. Architecturally, the contrast is more compelling than an attempt to make the addition disappear.

A Home That Climbs

The report identifies an original York stone staircase connecting the floors, plus a separate added elevator tower. Five bedrooms and four bathrooms occupy the tower. That vertical arrangement suggests a different domestic rhythm from a sprawling mansion, with movement between levels central to the design.

The Tank Becomes the Destination

At the top, Robb Report places the Prospect Room inside the original cast-iron water tank, with windows on all four sides and views including the London Eye. This is the conversion’s strongest idea: giving the tower’s defining industrial component a new role rather than treating it as a relic.
