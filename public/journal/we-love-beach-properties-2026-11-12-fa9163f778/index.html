We Love This Listing: Five concrete frames lift Lovell Beach House above the sand

At Newport Beach, R. M. Schindler arranged the living spaces around a two-storey view of the Pacific.

Published 2026-11-21. Original source date: 2026-11-12.

How to read the house from the street

Look for the gap beneath the upper rooms. R. M. Schindler carried the home on five concrete frames, raising the rooms where Philip and Leah Lovell spent their days above the sand. A tall grid of windows lets light into the double-height living room. The family could take in the Pacific from that room, then move up to the bedrooms along a projecting balcony. The Spaces reported an asking price of $18.8 million on November 12.

A coastal experiment revised

The house dates to 1926. In the original design, the sleeping porches were open to the air. Two years later Schindler enclosed them because the coastal fog made them too cold. Inside, a sofa and shelves he designed still give the dining and living areas their own boundaries. That piece of furniture solves at the scale of a room the same question the concrete frames address outside. What has to carry weight, and what can stay open?
