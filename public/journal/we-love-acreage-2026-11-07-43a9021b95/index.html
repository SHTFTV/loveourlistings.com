We Love This Listing: Borthwick Hall’s Baronial Scale and Nearly 12 Acres

Formal gardens, paddocks and a self-contained flat give this Scottish mansion an acreage story beyond its turrets.

Published 2026-11-12. Original source date: 2026-11-07.

Land with several roles

Country Life places Borthwick Hall in a secluded woodland valley south of Heriot, with just under 12 acres of formal gardens, grounds and paddocks. For us, that mix is the acreage appeal: not simply a large boundary, but land described in terms of different uses and characters.

Architecture with a signature

According to Country Life, architect John Henderson built the house in 1852. The publication describes harled walls, a bartizaned tower and crow-stepped gables. Those particulars make the Scottish Baronial label meaningful; our interest lies in the combination of a forceful silhouette and the domestic purpose behind it.

Space beyond the grand rooms

Country Life reports more than 11,000 square feet of living space, including a double-height reception hall, music room and cinema room. Our editorial pick is the contrast between ceremonial scale and rooms devoted to everyday enjoyment—a more interesting story than size alone.

A measure of independence

Of the seven bedrooms reported by Country Life, one forms part of a separate flat, with a spiral staircase connecting its living area to a bedroom mezzanine. We see that arrangement as a useful counterpoint to the mansion’s grandeur: a distinct retreat within a substantial shared home.
