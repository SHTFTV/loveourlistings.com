We Love This Listing: The arches at the Cloisters trace a garden path in Palm Springs

Liberace’s former desert home began as a bed-and-breakfast. Its walls enclose a series of courtyards.

Published 2026-11-25. Original source date: 2026-11-20.

With thanks

Desert Views Real Estate Photography via ELLE Decor

First published by ELLE Decor. Love Our Listings wrote this feature from that account.

Reading the garden at dusk

In the listing photograph, the first things to catch the light are the palms. Then the illuminated arches draw the eye across the lawn to the house. This series of sheltered passages and courtyards explains the name the Cloisters. The estate began as a Spanish Colonial Revival bed-and-breakfast in the 1930s. Liberace bought it in the late 1960s and lived here until his death in 1987.

After the show

The pianist furnished his Palm Springs rooms with gold accents and the candelabras associated with his stage shows. Brett Waterman led a later update, with the emphasis shifting to handcrafted details. The half-acre grounds offer more than a pretty approach to the front door. A heated pool and spa give the gardens places to pause, with the San Jacinto Mountains in view. ELLE Decor reported an asking price of nearly $6.9 million on November 20.
