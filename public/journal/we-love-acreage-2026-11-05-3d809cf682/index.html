We Love This Listing: Portmore’s Architecture, Gardens and Scottish Borders Acreage

A Baronial mansion and Victorian glasshouse anchor a 3,460-acre estate with several distinct landscapes.

Published 2026-11-18. Original source date: 2026-11-05.

An estate with several chapters

Country Life’s November 5 report describes Portmore as a 3,460-acre Scottish Borders estate, with offers over £15 million sought for the whole. Its centrepiece is a Category A-listed mansion designed by David Bryce in 1850. Seven estate houses, farmland, hill ground and woodland broaden the story beyond the main residence.

The garden has its own presence

The report highlights designed gardens and a Victorian glasshouse, alongside more than 17,200 square feet in the main house. A library and an organ room make the interior unusually specific. For us, the garden architecture is equally compelling: it provides another way to experience the estate’s scale through carefully composed places.

Why we love this listing

Portmore invites attention to how buildings, gardens and working land fit together. The reported lot options also make clear that an estate is a collection of responsibilities as well as rooms. That layered identity gives this acreage feature its appeal. Want to see your property here?
