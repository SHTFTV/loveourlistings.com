Luxury at a more human scale

A penthouse need not depend on a glass tower or a theatrical entrance to make an interesting proposition. PH6W at The Washington Irving in South Harlem puts a private roof garden at the centre of its appeal. Compass currently advertises the home at US$1.895 million; the listing specifies four bedrooms, two bathrooms and approximately 1,861 square feet. Checked October 29, that is an asking price, not a sale result. The feature that catches our attention is the invitation to make outdoor space part of everyday city living.

The roof as another room

The useful way to read a roof garden is through routine. Where would you have coffee? How would you carry a meal upstairs? Would the setting encourage a quiet hour outside, or only an occasional party? These questions are less glamorous than a skyline photograph, but more revealing. An outdoor space becomes valuable to its owner through the habits it supports. Our enthusiasm here is for that possibility, not a promise about sunshine, privacy or the condition of the roof.

Think about how the outdoor room will actually be used.

A layout with different rhythms

Four bedrooms allow a buyer to think beyond the single spectacular entertaining room. The interesting design exercise is how a home accommodates different days: guests visiting, work that needs a door, or people keeping separate schedules. None of that establishes permitted use or guarantees an easy alteration. It does explain why room distribution deserves attention alongside the headline view. A good viewing should test how the plan works for the household rather than merely confirming that the rooms exist.

Read the practical notes too

The broker’s description expressly notes that the gas fireplace needs re-lining. That small disclosure deserves to stay visible beside the more seductive roof-garden story. It is a reminder that character and maintenance arrive together. A buyer should ask the listing agent about that work, outdoor-space rights, building documents and current carrying costs, with appropriate independent advice. A film introduces a home’s atmosphere. It should never stand in for an inspection or for understanding what ownership will require.

Why we love it today

Today’s penthouse choice is about a particular version of urban luxury: room to live indoors and a reason to step outside without leaving home. It offers a quieter editorial counterpoint to the trophy-tower narrative. Watch Brian K. Lewis’s original property film and take practical questions directly to his Compass team.
