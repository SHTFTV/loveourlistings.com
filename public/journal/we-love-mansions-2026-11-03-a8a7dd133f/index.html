We Love This Listing: Shrubs Wood’s Remarkable Modernist Inheritance

A Buckinghamshire house where Mendelsohn and Chermayeff’s architecture, original furniture and garden history make a compelling whole.

Published 2026-11-12. Original source date: 2026-11-03.

A partnership preserved

Some houses deserve attention for the ideas they embody rather than their scale alone. Shrubs Wood, in Chalfont St Giles, is one such prospect. Country Life identifies it as a collaboration between Erich Mendelsohn and Serge Chermayeff, built in 1933–1934 and now Grade II*-listed. For us, that architectural authorship is the starting point of its appeal.

Concrete with a flourish

Country Life reports that the reinforced-concrete construction enabled large rooms, uninterrupted floor space and picture windows, alongside a quadrant-curve staircase. That combination is particularly engaging: structural ambition paired with a softer geometric gesture. The staircase offers a compelling counterpoint to the house’s largely rectangular plan.

More than an architectural shell

According to Country Life, the house contains 3,735 sq ft of internal space, with separate living and dining rooms and six first-floor bedrooms. The publication also reports that much of the furniture was designed specifically for the house by its architects. That continuity gives the interiors significance beyond a collection of period-compatible pieces.

A second creative chapter

Country Life credits Bridget D’Oyly Carte, who moved here in 1949, with transforming the five-acre grounds into gardens that are locally listed. Our reading of Shrubs Wood’s appeal is therefore twofold: a notable Modernist collaboration, complemented by a later landscape legacy rather than defined by architecture alone.
