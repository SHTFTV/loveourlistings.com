We Love This Listing: Mere Down House and Its Millennium Tower

A Wiltshire country house where a turn-of-the-century reinvention brings architectural ambition to silvery stone and Cornish slate.

Published 2026-11-12. Original source date: 2026-11-02.

A country house with a second chapter

Mere Down House’s appeal lies in the tension between its original construction and its later reinvention. Country Life reports that the Wiltshire property was built between 1920 and 1922 on Duchy of Cornwall land. That history offers a starting point, rather than the whole explanation for the house described today.

Restoration with a point of view

According to Country Life, the Grade II-listed house was almost completely rebuilt between 1999 and 2004, when the Millennium Tower and vine-clad loggia were added. To us, that makes the project interesting as an act of reinterpretation: a country house whose identity includes a substantial modern chapter.

Materials that hold the story together

Country Life describes local silvery stone beneath a Cornish slate roof, alongside antique marble flooring in the entrance hall and French oak boards in the sitting room. The editorial attraction is the material contrast—stone, slate, marble and timber offer plenty to consider without relying on sheer scale.

Beyond the principal rooms

The publication also reports a library, tower study, cutting garden and fruit trees. Those are the details we find most compelling: spaces associated with reading and growing give this architectural story a more domestic counterpoint.
