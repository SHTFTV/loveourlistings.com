An island built around gathering

The most interesting island properties are not always the ones promising the greatest distance from everybody else. Sometimes the appeal is having enough room to bring people together without asking them to share every moment. Picture Island is our island Real Estate We Love selection for that reason. Its separate main house and bunk house suggest a simple architectural question: where do you gather, and where do you retreat? This is our editorial pick, not a measured market ranking or an investment recommendation.

The offer, in the broker’s words and numbers

Rob Trembinski of EXIT Realty True North advertises Picture Island in Ontario’s Lake Huron North Channel as Available For Sale. His property page offers it for US$2,750,900 and describes C$3,800,000 as the approximate Canadian equivalent. The island is listed at 47.6 acres. The main house is described as 2,613 square feet, with one bedroom and two bathrooms; a separate bunk house adds four bedrooms and two bathrooms. These are listing representations checked on October 28, 2026. The current representative should confirm price, currency and availability before anyone makes plans.

Gather together. Retreat separately.

The main house and guest building serve different parts of an island stay.

Together, with somewhere to withdraw

The broker describes extensive connecting decks and a screened porch beside the main living spaces. For our purposes, the interesting idea is the relationship between those spaces. A gathering place works differently when visitors can leave the conversation without leaving the property. Watch the original brokerage film with that in mind: look for how the buildings relate to one another and to the shore. We have not visited, measured or inspected the island, and the film should be treated as the representative’s presentation rather than our independent survey.

The practical side belongs in the picture

According to the listing, the property has 400-amp electrical service supplied beneath the water and a floating dock in a sheltered cove. Those details raise useful ownership questions. Ask the agent what documentation is available for the service connection, dock, buildings and access arrangements. Discuss seasonal use, maintenance, insurance and the responsibilities attached to the land with qualified local professionals. A connection to the mainland is worth understanding as an operating arrangement, not merely celebrating as an amenity. The right questions help turn an island fantasy into a decision with its practical foundations visible.
