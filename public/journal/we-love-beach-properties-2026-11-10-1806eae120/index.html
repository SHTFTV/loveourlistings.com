We Love This Listing: A window seat faces the Pacific in Carl Maston’s Faria Beach house

A 1984 commission on Ventura’s coast takes its cues from the small summer cabins that once stood along the beach.

Published 2026-11-23. Original source date: 2026-11-10.

A house seen from the sand

From the beach, slim uprights divide a long bank of glass, and a tree stands between the house and its rail. Those narrow divisions make sense from the other side of the windows. Maston designed a built-in seat where someone can look out at the Pacific without moving a chair into place. Tracy Susman commissioned the three-bedroom house in 1984. The Spaces reported its first offering for sale on November 10.

What survives of the original design

Across its 2,640 square feet, Maston used cedar, exposed beams, concrete and brick. The roof changes level, giving the timber-lined interior a shape beyond that of a simple seaside box. Photographer Julius Shulman recorded the house in 1985. Those early pictures provide an unusual reference for reading what has survived, from the original materials to pieces of furniture designed to stay in place. The feature photograph here is by Sterling Reed.
