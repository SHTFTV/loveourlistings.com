We Love This Listing: West Stoke House, Where Georgian History Meets Motoring Design

A South Downs country house with a glass pool extension and an unexpected connection to the Rolls-Royce Ghost.

Published 2026-11-12. Original source date: 2026-11-12.

A substantial South Downs setting

Country Life describes West Stoke House as a Grade II-listed Georgian home in Chichester, with nine bedrooms and almost 11 acres within the South Downs National Park. That combination gives this feature its starting point: architectural history considered alongside the scale and setting of a country home.

A dining room with another role

According to Country Life, Rolls-Royce designers gathered here in 2006, when the property was a boutique hotel, using its dining room as a temporary studio. Those sessions produced the 200EX concept that became the Ghost. Editorially, this is the standout detail: a domestic room participating in industrial design history.

An older Goodwood connection

The motoring association is only one chapter. Country Life reports that Charles Lennox, the 2nd Duke of Richmond, purchased West Stoke House in 1764, bringing it into the wider Goodwood holdings. That earlier connection adds another historical layer without needing to lean on the car story alone.

Glass alongside Georgian

Country Life also reports a separate annexe, mature formal gardens and a rear swimming-pool extension made almost entirely of glass. On paper, the glass addition offers the most intriguing architectural contrast: a different material language alongside the Georgian house, rather than more period detail.
